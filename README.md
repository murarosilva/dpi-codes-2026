# Codes for the Manuscript: "Building First, Governing Later? Digital Public Infrastructure and the Power of Policy Narratives"
Victo Silva, Vinicius Muraro, Frederik Zuiderveen1, Raphael Gellert

## Abstract

Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) has rapidly emerged as a central policy concept across governments, international organizations, and think tanks, framed as a foundation for digital transformation, efficiency, and inclusive development. This article examines DPI as an emerging discursive order that structures how digital infrastructures are imagined and governed. Using computer-assisted text analysis of 712 DPI-related policy documents published between 2015 and 2025 and sourced from the Overton database, we analyze the relative salience of promotional and safeguarding narratives across organizations and over time. We find that DPI discourse is dominated by promotion-oriented framings, while legal, institutional, and technical safeguards for civil liberties remain marginal. Promotional narratives originate primarily in think tanks, are amplified by international organizations, and are adopted by governments, which rarely engage explicitly with risks or accountability concerns. We argue that this patterned imbalance constitutes a discursively produced accountability gap, through which governance arrangements are prefigured before safeguards are institutionalized. By conceptualizing DPI as a narrative technology of governance, the article contributes to critical debates on digital infrastructure, state capacity, and the political economy of data in the digital age.
